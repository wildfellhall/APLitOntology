# Verified patterns and sources

Reviewed November 1, 2026.

- [College Board CED, effective fall 2024](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-english-literature-and-composition-course-and-exam-description.pdf): six big ideas, seven skill categories, 31 coded skills; recurring short-fiction, poetry, and longer-fiction/drama units. The actual table on printed page 17 distinguishes three setting skills from four narrator skills; search-result extraction can scramble columns and fabricate duplicate codes. Use the source table and unit skill descriptions.
- [Exam overview](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-english-literature-and-composition/exam): 55 MCQs in 60 minutes (45%), three essays in 120 minutes (55%); five passage sets include at least two prose/drama and two poetry selections. The live page describes digital delivery.
- CED sample items, printed pages 139–145: four options; examples ask about negative diction, narrative shifts, figurative relationships, narrator perspective, and a passage’s structure. They test an interpretation of a particular choice. The literary-argument sample asks for a feature’s contribution to the work as a whole, not a general social-policy argument.
- [2026 released FRQs](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap26-frq-english-literature.pdf): contextualized poem and prose passage, followed by an open literary argument. Original tasks should emulate architecture without reproducing these copyrighted prompts or licensed contemporary passages.
- [2026 scoring guidelines](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap26-sg-english-literature.pdf): task-specific decisions within the 1/4/1 dimensions; sustained explanation and warranted complexity matter more than a device count.
- [Purdue OWL, writing about poetry](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/writing_in_literature/writing_about_poetry.html): useful guidance for connecting formal observations to a textually supported interpretation.
- [Carnegie Mellon, rubrics](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/rubrics.html): explicit analytic criteria and observable performance levels.
- [Project Gutenberg license](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html): retain the original files and license notices when distributing Gutenberg-attributed texts; credit edition and translator. Public-domain status is stated for the United States on each catalog record.

Design inference: a strong short set moves among local detail, relationships, and the whole without assuming that those moves must occur in a fixed order. A distractor may describe something true but misstate its function. The strongest creative projects change one literary variable, then require students to test what that change does to interpretation.

## Lessons from building a complete library

Use an edition manifest and reproducible local extraction rather than memory when writing about punctuation, line counts, stanza counts, or a play's wording. Early Dickinson editions can omit stanzas or alter punctuation; the displayed poem governs the question. A collection's title-page year does not necessarily date each poem. Identify prose speeches in drama rather than treating every wrapped row as verse.

Count canonical tasks at the requested concept level. A book page may gather existing questions by source without increasing the unique count. Keep source context, paragraph or verse numbers, and choice explanations available beside the task.

Review distractors for plausible mistaken relationships, not just incorrect events. Replace alternatives such as an invented new narrator or an absent ceremony with a credible misreading of scope, function, or agency. Check both unusually long and unusually short keys; mechanical equalization can create a new cue. A length audit flags review candidates and cannot validate a question.

Make whole-work project reading requirements explicit. A collection project can assign named complete poems or stories; a novel argument requires the complete novel. Preserve the difference between invention, adaptation, historical context, and textual evidence in the directions and rubric.
